## The story as a first-time listener hears it

I heard it in order without the bible, and I could follow the story all the way through.

One Tuesday in October, a hand surgeon called Mae is late for school with her daughter Wren and cuts a bagel held in her left palm, against her own famous advice. The knife cuts the deep flexor tendon of her ring finger in Zone Two. The two ends are characters. Prox is the King's daughter; the King is the forearm muscle. Tip is her husband, the fingertip end. When the tendon is cut, the King yanks Prox back into the palm and Tip slides away toward the fingertip. Lumbo the lumbrical stops Prox at the palm, and the King's pulling straightens the finger, which is lumbrical-plus. Prox realises "we were two" and decides to go back for Tip. Surgery is set for Thursday, day two.

Day one: the pulleys panic about being vented. Tip admits he has never moved by himself. A1 introduces Scarlett Adhesion, who "moves into any space that stops moving".

Day two: Teo operates under local anaesthetic (WALANT). He uses a zig-zag incision, fetches Prox with a children's feeding tube and pins her with a needle. He does a six-strand repair plus a running stitch, vents a sliver of A4, checks the fist while Mae is awake, and repairs Digby, the ulnar digital nerve. Mae goes home in plaster.

Day three: Thea the hand therapist makes a dorsal blocking splint. Her rules are hourly place-and-hold, don't tear it, don't glue it, and the King must pull "with a whisper". Day ten: the skin stitches come out. Mae grabs Wren on the stairs with the splinted hand and the repair nearly ruptures. The King swears never to pull again, Tip is afraid to move, Mae skips her exercises and Scarlett appears. Act one ends on a cliffhanger: "tear or glue?"

Weeks three and four: Mae lies to Thea about doing the exercises and Scarlett glues Tip down with type III collagen. Digby regrows about a millimetre a day, tracked by Thea's tapping (Tinel's sign). Wren now cuts the bagel herself. At week six the splint comes off. The fingertip won't bend, and the middle and little fingers stop short too (the quadriga effect). At week eight Teo says "stuck, not snapped" and mentions a tenolysis. Mae refuses one and goes back to hourly exercises. Prox teaches the King to pull gently, Tip asks to be pulled, and the tip moves five degrees. Week twelve, January, the first day of term: Mae makes a full fist around Wren's hand, Scarlett has remodelled to type I, and the show closes on "Make a fist for me. Now open."

The places I briefly lost track were:
- Who "Big Sister" is.
- Why the King is pulling again at week six.
- "I nearly had her on the stairs".
- The ring that was cut off and then simply "slides back".

It does sound like one modern pop musical. The shared sound signatures (GLIDE shimmer, TAP double tick, Scarlett's sub-bass and muted trumpet, the ticking piano at the start and end) hold it together, and the genres are well varied: pop-orchestral opener, piano power ballad, chamber patter, 6/8 acoustic duet, bubblegum cheer-pop, pop-rock act-one finale, electro-swing villain number, country-pop, gospel eleven-o'clock number, and a stomp-clap finale.

The motif lines are verbatim and consistent:
- "Through the sheath and past the pulleys, one line from the elbow to the fingertip" (cut off on purpose in songs 1, 2, 3 and 6)
- "Make a fist for me. Now open."
- "I never knew that we were two / until there was a space between"
- "A little further every day, a millimetre, more or less"
- "Hold still, hold still, nothing tears if nothing moves"
- "Every hour", "Tear it, or glue it", "Place and hold, and then let go", "Tap, tap"

The timeline adds up: Tuesday plus two days is Thursday, week four lands in November, week eight before Christmas, week twelve in January. The medical details are sharp and land for hand surgeons, including lumbrical-plus, Bunnell's No Man's Land, WALANT, the feeding-tube retrieval, the A4 vent, the quadriga effect, Tinel's sign and type III to type I collagen.

Nothing is preachy. The moral stays hinted ("not like Day Ten, not like never, like this"; "glide on the space between"). The only near-echo of a famous line is "We are not animals!" in Song 3, which is low risk. "The space between" is also the title of an existing pop song, but only as a phrase; nothing is quoted.

The biggest technical risk is Song 5. It uses inline speaker labels ("THEA:", "GIRLS:", "MAE:", "KING:", "ALL:", "PROX:"), and Suno will often sing those aloud. On length: Song 7's lyrics are 4,827 characters, under 5,000 but above the 3,000–4,600 target, and Song 10's style is 995 of 1,000 characters.
