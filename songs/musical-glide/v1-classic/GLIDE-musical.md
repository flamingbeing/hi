# GLIDE: A Fairytale in Twelve Weeks

*On a rushed October school morning, Mae, a hand surgeon, won't let her daughter cut her own bagel. Then she cuts it herself, held in the palm of her left hand. Every patient has heard her lecture against doing exactly that. The knife cuts the deep flexor tendon of her ring finger in Zone Two, No Man's Land, and divides a couple so long married that they had forgotten they were two. PROX, the forearm King's daughter, is dragged back into the dark palm by her father. TIP, the fingertip's boy, slides away towards the end of the world. A surgeon Mae trained stitches them back together. Then they have twelve weeks to learn to GLIDE. If the King pulls too hard, they tear. If they hold too still, they belong to Scarlett Adhesion, the honey-voiced scar who purrs that nothing tears if nothing moves.*

## Recurring motifs

- **THE SPACE BETWEEN (with its ONE LINE verse couplet)**: "Through the sheath and past the pulleys,
one line from the elbow to the fingertip.
I never knew that we were two
until there was a space between."
  Two couplets used separately. The first is the ONE LINE couplet (lines 1-2) and the second is the SPACE couplet (lines 3-4). Song 1: Prox and Tip sing the ONE LINE couplet EXACTLY, in unison, as a boast. The third time it breaks off after 'one line from the elbow to the-' at the knife. Song 2: Prox sings the ONE LINE couplet alone with the last word missing ('one line from the elbow to the...'), then introduces the SPACE couplet exactly as the chorus hook. The space is the injury. Song 3: the Pulleys mangle the ONE LINE couplet ('Through the sheath and past the- us, dear. US.'). Song 4: the ONE LINE couplet is sung exactly in HARMONY. The SPACE couplet is sung exactly, then bent to 'Now there's barely a space between, / three millimetres, never more.' Song 5: Thea redefines the space (spoken or sung): 'The space between that matters now / is you and the tunnel wall, a drop of water wide.' Song 7: Scarlett mocks it: 'There won't be any space between. / I'll fill it, darling.' Song 9: Prox bends it to resolve: 'I never knew that we were two / so I'll keep open the space between.' This means keeping the glide space free, never closing a gap. Song 10: the final form is 'Now we know that we are two, / and we glide on the space between.', with the ONE LINE couplet exact in two-part harmony as the finger makes a full fist.
- **HOLD STILL**: "Hold still, hold still,
nothing tears if nothing moves."
  Song 1 (seed, human): Mae, rushing, says to Wren 'Wren, hold still!' (sun cream). It is a parent's everyday phrase, sung and spoken. Song 6: the full couplet, exact and first heard faintly from Scarlett just after the near-tear, when it sounds like comfort and almost like wisdom. Mae can murmur 'hold still' to her own hand just before it. Song 7: the exact couplet as Scarlett's full chorus hook. Variant 'Hold still, Doctor' sung up to Mae. Song 9: Scarlett sings it exactly. Thea answers 'Not hold still. Place and hold, / and then let go.' (planted in Song 5 as 'Place and hold, and then let go.'). Tip turns it: 'Nothing tears if nothing moves, / but nothing ever moves.' Song 10: Mae tying Wren's shoe at the gate: 'Hold still... done. Go on.' The villain's line returns as a mother's ordinary words, followed by release. It is never explained.
- **MAKE A FIST / NOW OPEN**: "Make a fist for me.
Now open."
  Song 1: the chorus boasts that Mae says 'Make a fist for me. Now open.' to a thousand hands. After the cut she self-tests: 'Hold the middle joint. Bend the tip. Nothing.' Song 4: Teo says 'Make a fist for me.' and the repair catches on A4, which is vented. Again: Mae 'I've said that to a thousand patients.' Teo: 'Then you know the next bit. Now open.' It glides. Song 8 (final verse): Thea says 'Make a fist for me.' when the splint comes off, and nothing happens. Song 9: Thea says 'Make a fist for me.' paired with the blocking exercise 'Hold the middle joint. Bend just the tip.' (which echoes Mae's Song 1 self-test), and the first flicker comes. Song 10: the last words of the show, sung softly by Mae to herself as an absent-minded rehab rep after Wren runs in, exactly 'Make a fist for me. / Now open.'
- **A LITTLE FURTHER**: "A little further every day,
a millimetre, more or less."
  Song 8: Digby's deadpan, shrugging road-trip chorus about his own nerve regrowth. At the end of the song Prox sings it alone, slow and sad, after the failed fist. Song 9: Prox takes it as the eleven-o'clock hook, the method for working Tip free, sung first slow and then triumphant after the key change. Song 10: Digby's arrival joke: 'A little further every day... / and here I am. More or less.'
- **PULL WITH A WHISPER (the King's motif)**: "Pull with a whisper, Your Majesty."
  Song 5: Thea's comic prescription to the King, who wants to show off his strength. Song 6: forgotten. He pulls with everything ('I've got her!') and Prox cries 'Not like that, Papa!' Song 7: twisted into his frozen vow, 'No pull at all. Not even a whisper.' Scarlett: 'Never pull again, Your Majesty.' Song 9: Prox teaches it back: 'Not like Day Ten. Not like never. Like this.' The King, wondering: 'A whisper. Like this?' Song 10: the King, proud: 'I pull with a whisper now.' All three of his daughters close.

## The story as a first-time listener hears it

I listened as a first-timer, in order, and could follow the whole story without the bible. Song 1: on an October school morning, inside the left hand of Doctor Mae, a proud kingdom introduces its King in the forearm and the fused couple 'Proxantip'. Mae won't let Wren cut her own bagel, cuts it in her palm herself, and the unison breaks. The King drags Prox up into the palm, Tip slides away down the finger, and it is Zone Two. Song 2: in the dark palm Prox realises they were two all along. A fretful little muscle stopped her slipping further (the lumbrical-plus gag lands). She refuses her father's 'come home' and vows to go back for Tip, with the surgery on Thursday. Song 3: the pulleys panic about being vented. Tip turns out vain and charming, then admits he has never moved by himself. A spooky aside names Scarlett Adhesion, who 'moves into any space that stops moving'. Song 4: in a wide-awake operation, Teo (Mae trained him) rejoins them with six strands. The repair catches on A4, he vents it, Mae hears her own 'Make a fist / Now open' and it glides. The nerve is mended last and she goes home in plaster. Song 5: the therapist Thea fits the splint, gives the rules and the 'tear it or glue it' dilemma, and tells the King to 'pull with a whisper'. Song 6: on Day Ten Mae grabs Wren on the stairs, the King yanks, and the repair nearly tears. That night the King swears never to pull again, Tip is scared, Mae thinks of skipping her exercises, and Scarlett's 'hold still' drifts in. Cliffhanger. Song 7: Mae lies at clinic. Scarlett seduces Tip, the guilty King and Mae herself, and Tip is glued to the wall. Song 8: Digby the nerve's slow road trip, Wren cutting the bagel on the board while Mae lets her, then the splint comes off at six weeks and the fingertip won't bend. Song 9: at week eight Teo calls it 'stuck, not snapped' and floats a tenolysis. Mae recommits. Prox stops her father ripping Tip loose and teaches him the whisper, Tip asks to be pulled anyway, and the angle climbs from five degrees to forty. Song 10: in January, on the first day of term, comes a full fist round Wren's hand. Everyone gets a callback, Scarlett is remodelled, and Mae ties a shoe ('Hold still... done. Go on.'), lets Wren run in, and quietly does one rep. Things that are fine: the motifs are verbatim where they should be, there are no 'darling' leaks outside Scarlett, and the timeline holds (Tuesday Day 0, Thursday Day 2, the November lollies, a New Year nerve arrival, a January finale), as do the six strands and the A4-only vent. The voice descriptions are largely consistent, and every style prompt is under 1000 characters (the longest are about 930). Places where I got briefly lost or something is weak: (a) Lumbo is never named in a sung line until Song 10's 'thank you, Lumbo!', so that payoff is a stranger's name. (b) Song 1 never says aloud that Mae is a hand surgeon. (c) Thea's 'Stitched is Tuesday' sounds like a timeline error, because the surgery was on a Thursday. (d) The 'wand' that reads degrees is never set up as the goniometer, so 'five degrees' in Song 9 works only on inference, and Scarlett's 'little plastic wand' means nothing to families. (e) The 'SISTERS' girl voices in Song 8 may not render as distinct singers in Suno. Nothing reads as preachy. The nearest is Song 6's final chorus, which spells out the tear-or-glue dichotomy, but it is framed as medical stakes and it works. Lyric lengths look to be inside 5000 characters, but Songs 5 and 6 are the longest and should be measured exactly before generating.
