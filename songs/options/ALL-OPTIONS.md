## Option 02: The Eponymous Examiner
*A Victorian comic-opera patter song. A pompous consultant hand surgeon races through every eponymous sign and test he knows (Finkelstein/Eichhoff, Phalen, Durkan, Tinel, Froment, Jeanne, three Wartenbergs, Allen, Elson, Watson, Kanavel, then a blur of Bunnell, Bouvier, Egawa, Linburg-Comstock, Masse, Pitres-Testut and Duchenne). The orchestra speeds up with each verse and the company chorus repeats his last line back at him. Then the music stops dead, he cannot remember the patient's name, gives the WHO checklist a ray instead of a name, and finally guesses "Baron Dupuytren".*

Oom-pah tuba and bassoon on the downbeats, bright brass fanfares, staccato strings, clarinet runs, piccolo flourishes, woodblock and triangle, cymbal crashes on chorus cadences. Lead vocal is a clear, crisp, classically trained comic baritone with very fast, precise diction, theatrical and pompous. A mixed company chorus answers each verse by repeating its last line in robust unison. Mock-tragic minor-key bridge with weeping violins and rubato. Spoken asides cut in over a stopped orchestra. Bouncy 2/4 march feel, operetta harmony, dry concert-hall acoustic, warm vintage stage-show mix with the vocal forward and every consonant audible. Ends on a grand ritardando, a final brass chord and a short tuba button.

Jokes hand surgeons will catch:

(2) "Grayson's on the ground... Cleland's on the ceiling": the standard mnemonic. Grayson's ligament (palmar) becomes part of the spiral cord, while Cleland's (dorsal) is spared.

- The awkward "Cleland's still stands" became the ground/ceiling mnemonic, which is anatomically correct.
- "Three operations" became "Needle, scalpel, skin graft", since needle fasciotomy is a procedure rather than a full operation.
