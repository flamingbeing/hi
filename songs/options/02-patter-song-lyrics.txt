[Intro | Pompous brass fanfare, oom-pah tuba vamp, pit orchestra, spoken baritone]
(Spoken) Ahem. Nurse, the two-point discriminator.
No, no. The paper clip.

[Verse 1 | Crisp comic baritone patter, moderate tempo, staccato diction, tuba on the beat]
I am a hand examiner of eponymous proficiency,
I've signs for every ligament and every nerve's deficiency,
I'll Finkelstein your thumb until it's radially regretful,
Then call it the Eichhoff, since the purists get so fretful.
I'll Phalen you for sixty seconds, wrists in full flexion,
Then Durkan you for thirty, with my thumbs in firm compression,
I'll Tinel-tap your nerve repair and chase it down the way,
A millimetre daily, so I'll see you back next May!

[Chorus | Full company chorus, pompous unison, brass swells, cymbal crash]
He'll see you back next May, he'll see you back next May,
A millimetre daily, so he'll see you back next May!

[Verse 2 | Baritone patter quickens, clarinet runs, woodblock clicks, cheeky]
I'll have you pinch a sheet of paper, then I'll tug it free,
Your thumb I-P flexes, that's the F-P-L, you see,
Your adductor pollicis has packed its bags and left the scene,
And when your thumb M-P hyperextends, I'll holler "Jeanne!"
Your little finger drifting out? It's Wartenberg, I'll tell,
Not Wartenberg's syndrome, that's the handcuff nerve. Oh, hell!
There's even one that migrates, Wartenberg was versatile,
But Froment's is the paper one, I'll tug it with a smile!

[Chorus | Full company chorus, brighter, tuba bounce, triangle ting]
He'll tug it with a smile, he'll tug it with a smile,
For Froment's is the paper one, he'll tug it with a smile!

[Verse 3 | Faster baritone patter, breathless, strings stabbing, tempo pushing]
I'll Allen-test your wrist: I squeeze both arteries, you pump your fist,
Release the ulnar, if it pinks, your radial won't be missed!
I'll Elson-test your P-I-P at ninety off the table's edge,
If D-I-P goes rigid, then your central slip's gone off the ledge!
I'll Watson-shift your scaphoid till I feel a mighty clunk,
Which proves a scapholunate tear, or proves you're young and loose and drunk,
For one in three of normal folk will clunk the same, I fear,
So I'll M-R-A it, scope it, and then argue for a year!

[Chorus | Full company chorus, rowdy, brass blaring, foot stomps]
He'll argue for a year, he'll argue for a year,
He'll M-R-A it, scope it, and then argue for a year!

[Verse 4 | Breakneck baritone patter, accelerando, barely breathing, orchestra racing]
Kanavel's four I'll rattle off before you've even sat:
The finger held in slight flexion, the swelling sausage-fat,
The sheath is tender all along, and passive stretch? The worst,
The earliest and loudest, so I'll book the washout first!
Then Bunnell, Bouvier, Egawa, Linburg-Comstock, Masse,
Pitres-Testut, Duchenne, the grind, I'll test you till you pass,
I'll book you nerve conduction, and the wait is nine months long,
I'll read it once you're cured and swear that I was never wrong!

[Break | Orchestra stops dead, single tuba note, spoken baritone, puzzled]
(Spoken) Now then. And you are...?
Mrs... um... the left carpal tunnel. Bay three.
No. That's the trapeziectomy. Tuesday.

[Bridge | Slow mock-tragic baritone, minor key, weeping violins, rubato]
I know the Tinel, Phalen, Durkan, Froment, Jeanne and Allen,
I know your scaphoid's blood runs backward, proximal pole failin',
I'd know your X-ray anywhere, I'd know your Dupuytren's cord,
But when the checklist asked your name, I said "Fourth ray. Left. Cord."

[Final Verse | Tempo snaps back, full oom-pah, baritone triumphant then sheepish]
So I'm the very paragon of eponymous examination,
With a sign for every tendon, nerve and wrist articulation,
From Paris, Philly, Tokyo, the surnames fill my brain,
But I've marked the arrow on your arm, and can't recall your name!

[Final Chorus | Full company chorus, fortissimo, grand ritardando, final brass chord]
He can't recall your name, he can't recall your name,
He's marked the arrow on your arm, and can't recall your name!
(Spoken) ...Baron... Dupuytren?

[Outro | Oom-pah tag, tuba button, cymbal crash, applause]
